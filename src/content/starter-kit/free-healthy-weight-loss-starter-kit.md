# Meal Plan Tips

You don't need to follow this plan perfectly to see results.

Instead, use it as inspiration for building balanced meals that fit your lifestyle.

If you don't like a specific ingredient, simply replace it with another food from the same category.

For example:

- Chicken ↔ Turkey
- Salmon ↔ Tuna
- Greek yogurt ↔ Cottage cheese
- Brown rice ↔ Quinoa
- Blueberries ↔ Strawberries

Consistency matters much more than perfection.

The goal isn't to eat the "perfect" meal every time.

The goal is to make healthy choices more often than unhealthy ones.

# Shopping Tips

Before going to the store:

✔ Check what you already have.

✔ Prepare a shopping list.

✔ Never shop while hungry.

✔ Buy mostly whole foods.

✔ Read nutrition labels.

✔ Choose foods with higher protein and fiber.

✔ Avoid buying foods "just in case."

---

# Healthy Shopping Rule

A simple rule that works for most people:

Spend about 80% of your shopping time around the outer aisles of the supermarket, where you'll usually find fresh vegetables, fruit, meat, fish, dairy, and eggs.

Visit the middle aisles only for healthy pantry staples like oats, rice, beans, spices, and canned foods.

This simple habit can naturally reduce the amount of highly processed foods you bring home.

# Healthy Portion Guide

One of the biggest challenges when trying to lose weight is knowing how much to eat.

The good news?

You don't need to weigh every meal or count every calorie.

A simple hand-portion method works surprisingly well for most people and can be used almost anywhere.

![The Perfect Healthy Plate](/weight-loss-without-starving/images/starter-kit/healthy-plate-infographic.png)

The Healthy Plate method makes balanced eating simple. Fill half your plate with vegetables, one quarter with lean protein, and one quarter with healthy carbohydrates. Add a small serving of healthy fats and drink plenty of water to create satisfying, nutrient-dense meals that support long-term weight loss.

![Healthy Portion Guide](/weight-loss-without-starving/images/starter-kit/healthy-portion-guide-infographic.png)

The hand portion method makes estimating serving sizes simple without weighing food. Use your palm for protein, two fists for vegetables, one cupped hand for healthy carbohydrates, and one thumb for healthy fats to build balanced meals anywhere.

---

# The Hand Portion Method

## 🥩 Protein = One Palm

Examples:

- Chicken breast
- Turkey
- Fish
- Lean beef
- Tofu
- Cottage cheese

Aim for **1–2 palm-sized portions** at each main meal.

---

## 🥦 Vegetables = Two Fists

Fill at least half of your plate with vegetables.

Good choices include:

- Broccoli
- Spinach
- Cauliflower
- Green beans
- Peppers
- Cucumbers
- Tomatoes
- Zucchini

The more colorful your vegetables are, the better.

---

## 🍚 Healthy Carbohydrates = One Cupped Hand

Examples:

- Brown rice
- Quinoa
- Oats
- Sweet potatoes
- Whole wheat pasta
- Whole grain bread

Carbohydrates provide energy, but portion size matters.

---

## 🥑 Healthy Fats = One Thumb

Examples:

- Olive oil
- Avocado
- Nuts
- Seeds
- Peanut butter

Healthy fats are important, but because they are calorie-dense, smaller portions are usually enough.

---

# Build a Balanced Plate

A simple healthy plate looks like this:

🥦 50% Vegetables

🥩 25% Lean Protein

🍚 25% Healthy Carbohydrates

🥑 Add a small serving of healthy fats

This balance provides protein, fiber, vitamins, minerals, and long-lasting energy.

---

# Simple Portion Tips

✔ Eat slowly.

✔ Stop when you're comfortably full—not stuffed.

✔ Drink water before meals.

✔ Use smaller plates if you tend to overeat.

✔ Focus on protein and vegetables first.

✔ Enjoy treats occasionally without guilt.

Remember:

Healthy eating is about consistency, not perfection.

# Protein Cheat Sheet

Protein is one of the most important nutrients for healthy weight loss.

It helps you stay full longer, supports muscle maintenance, and can make losing weight easier by reducing hunger.

Aim for **25–35 grams of protein** at most main meals.

# Protein Goal Calculator

A simple guideline:

- Sedentary adults:
  **1.0–1.2 g per kg of body weight**

- Active adults:
  **1.2–1.6 g per kg**

- Building muscle:
  **1.6–2.2 g per kg**
